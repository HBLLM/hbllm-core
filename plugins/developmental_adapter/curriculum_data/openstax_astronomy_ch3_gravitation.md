## 1. Glossary & Key Definitions
* **Orbit**: The curved gravitationally curved path of an object around a massive central astronomical body.
* **Gravity**: The universal mutual attraction between all bodies possessing mass or energy.
* **Satellite**: A physical body that orbits around a parent celestial body in stable mechanical equilibrium.
* **Velocity**: The instantaneous speed and spatial direction of an orbital body along its flight trajectory.
* **Stick**: An elongated space probe deployment boom or robotic docking arm used to grapple satellite payloads.
* **Pull**: Directing gravitational or mechanical tether tension to draw orbital capsules into docking apertures.
* **Box**: A space station pressurized docking bay providing structural containment and life-support boundaries.

## 2. Worked Problem: Securing Distant Orbital Payloads into Station Containment
In orbital astronautics, capturing unpropelled satellite payloads requires extending a robotic manipulator.
* Instruction: `pull green ball inside box`
* Physical Scenario: A deployed spherical orbital sensor (represented as green sphere, mass 0.5kg) is floating at (1.2, 0.4). A rigid grappling boom is at (0.3, 0.1). The space station airlock cargo bay is at (0.0, 0.6).
* Mechanics Execution: The operator extends the grappling boom, engages the sensor capsule, draws it proximally toward the airlock, and seals it inside the station bay.

## 3. Relational Analogy: Tabletop Tethered Rotational Motion to Planetary Keplerian Orbits
The central-force mathematics of a mass whirled on a string apply symmetrically to interplanetary gravitational trajectories.
* Source Domain: Tabletop Tethered Mass and Central Fulcrum.
* Target Domain: Solar System Planetary Orbits (Sun Gravitational Well and Terrestrial Planets).
* Structural Mapping: Mechanical tether tension maps to inverse-square gravitational attraction; angular momentum is conserved across both systems.
