## 1. Glossary & Key Definitions
* **Charge**: An intrinsic physical property of matter that causes it to experience electromagnetic force.
* **Conductor**: A material containing mobile charge carriers that readily redistribute under an external electric field.
* **Insulator**: A material whose internal electric charges do not move freely, preventing electric current.
* **Coulomb**: The standard SI unit of electric charge, equal to the charge conveyed by one ampere in one second.
* **Stick**: An insulated dielectric rod used to safely reposition charged electrostatic test spheres.
* **Push**: Applying normal mechanical force to displace like-charge test masses against repulsive electrostatic gradients.
* **Box**: A grounded metallic Faraday enclosure that shields its interior volume from external electrostatic fields.

## 2. Worked Problem: Guiding Electrostatic Test Charges into Shielded Faraday Enclosures
When charged particles generate intense electrostatic fields, they must be contained inside a grounded Faraday cage.
* Instruction: `push red ball inside box`
* Physical Scenario: A positively charged red metallic sphere (charge +2uC, mass 0.5kg) is placed at (0.8, 0.2). An open grounded Faraday shield container is located at (0.0, 0.6).
* Mechanics Execution: The operator uses an insulated tool, advances behind the charged mass, overcomes electrostatic deflection, and secures the charge inside the Faraday shielding box.
