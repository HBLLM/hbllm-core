## 1. Glossary & Key Definitions
* **Force**: An interaction that causes an acceleration of a body with mass when unopposed.
* **Mass**: A quantitative measure of inertia and resistance of an object to acceleration.
* **Friction**: A contact resistance force opposing relative motion between surfaces in contact.
* **Inertia**: The tendency of a physical object to resist changes in its velocity.
* **Push**: An applied compressive mechanical force directed away from the actuator along a surface.
* **Box**: A rigid enclosed structural vessel capable of containing and securing displaced mass.

## 2. Worked Problem: Overcoming Surface Friction to Displace Loads
When a resting load has mass and contacts a frictional surface, an applied pushing force must overcome static resistance.
* Instruction: `push red ball inside box`
* Physical Scenario: A red ball load (mass 0.5kg) rests at position (0.8, 0.2). An open storage box is located at (0.0, 0.6). The table friction coefficient opposes initial velocity.
* Mechanics Execution: The operator positions behind the mass, applies compressive normal force along the trajectory towards the container, accelerates the mass into proximal reach, and deposits it inside the container.

## 3. Relational Analogy: Surface Sliding Friction to Industrial Freight Haulage
The mechanical conservation laws governing contact friction and inertia scale across all terrestrial transport systems.
* Source Domain: Tabletop Sliding Mass and Container.
* Target Domain: Heavy Freight Haulage and Deceleration Buffer (50-ton railcar entering hydraulic capture basin).
* Structural Mapping: Applied normal force and surface resistance map symmetrically to locomotive brake shoes and rail arrestors.
