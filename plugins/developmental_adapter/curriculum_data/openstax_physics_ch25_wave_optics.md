## 1. Glossary & Key Definitions
* **Refraction**: The change in propagation direction of a wave front entering a medium with different phase velocity.
* **Prism**: A transparent optical element with flat, polished surfaces that refract light at designated angles.
* **Dispersion**: The wavelength dependence of phase velocity causing spectral separation of polychromatic beams.
* **Lens**: A transmissive optical device that focuses or disperses light beams through refraction.
* **Push**: Directing a precision alignment force along an optical track to seat optical elements into collimation cavities.
* **Box**: A light-tight spectrophotometer chassis housing dispersed spectral detectors and reference apertures.

## 2. Worked Problem: Optical Collimation and Detector Aperture Alignment
In optical instrumentation, precision alignment requires seating spherical focusing elements into darkroom receivers.
* Instruction: `push blue ball inside box`
* Physical Scenario: A spherical sapphire ball lens (represented as blue sphere, refractive index 1.77, mass 0.5kg) is at (0.8, 0.2). A light-shielded spectrometer collimator box is at (0.0, 0.6).
* Mechanics Execution: The operator positions behind the optical element, pushes it along the bench guide rails, and nests it securely inside the detector box aperture.

## 3. Relational Analogy: Tabletop Prism Spectrograph to High-Resolution Astronomical Echelle Spectrometer
