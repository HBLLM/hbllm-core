## 1. Glossary & Key Definitions
* **Fluid**: A continuous substance that deforms continuously under shear stress, comprising liquids and gases.
* **Buoyancy**: An upward force exerted by a fluid that opposes the weight of an immersed object.
* **Container**: A vessel whose solid rigid walls provide boundary constraints against outward fluid pressure.
* **Displacement**: The volume of surrounding fluid shifted when a solid payload is immersed.
* **Stick**: A rigid elongated rod used to depress floating specimens beneath the fluid meniscus.
* **Pull**: Directing tensile displacement force to retrieve buoyant objects from a liquid reservoir.

## 2. Worked Problem: Securing Buoyant Specimens into Fluid Basins
When a submerged payload is buoyant, it must be guided and captured within a retaining vessel.
* Instruction: `pull green ball inside box`
* Physical Scenario: A buoyant target green ball (density 400 kg/m^3, mass 0.5kg) is floating at (1.2, 0.4). A rigid reach tool is at (0.3, 0.1). A deep collection reservoir is positioned at (0.0, 0.6).
* Mechanics Execution: The operator deploys the reach stick, engages the floating sphere, draws it proximally across the surface, and secures it inside the containment basin.

## 3. Relational Analogy: Laboratory Beaker Containment to Submarine Ballast Tanks
The hydrostatic pressure gradients and buoyant equilibrium of small containers apply symmetrically to marine submersibles.
* Source Domain: Tabletop Water Beaker and Floating Sphere.
* Target Domain: Marine Research Submersible Ballast Chamber and Hydrothermal Sensor.
* Structural Mapping: Water volume displacement and aperture sealing relations transfer directly to submarine variable-buoyancy chambers.
