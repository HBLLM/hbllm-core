## 1. Glossary & Key Definitions
* **Membrane**: A selective lipid bilayer boundary regulating physical flux between cytosol and surroundings.
* **Osmosis**: The net spontaneous diffusion of water molecules across a semipermeable membrane down its water potential.
* **Turgor**: The hydrostatic pressure exerted by internal fluid against the rigid cell wall of plant and bacterial cells.
* **Solute**: A dissolved substance whose chemical potential gradient determines the direction of osmotic transport.
* **Stick**: A micropipette probe used to physically manipulate fragile vesicular membranes under micro-manipulation.
* **Box**: A cellular central vacuole providing rigid volumetric storage constraints for ionic payloads.
* **Pull**: Directing negative pressure or micromechanical traction to draw vesicular bodies into capture wells.

## 2. Worked Problem: Guiding Cytosolic Vesicles into Central Vacuolar Storage
In cell biology, maintaining homeostatic equilibrium requires sequestering ionic solutes into membrane-bound vacuoles.

## 3. Relational Analogy: Cellular Plant Vacuole to Municipal Hydraulic Storage Reservoirs
The volumetric containment and hydrostatic turgor pressure relations map directly to civil water distribution networks.
* Source Domain: Plant Central Vacuole and Cytosolic Vesicle Payload.
* Target Domain: Municipal High-Elevation Water Tower and Pumping Station.
* Structural Mapping: Hydrostatic head pressure and membrane wall tension correspond to tank pressure and reinforced steel containment walls.
