## 1. Glossary & Key Definitions
* **Atom**: The fundamental structural unit of matter comprising a dense central nucleus surrounded by orbiting electrons.
* **Nucleus**: The extremely dense, positively charged central core containing protons and neutrons.
* **Box**: A rigid boundary or potential well that confines particle movement within specific spatial coordinates.
* **Stick**: An instrumentation probe or directional magnetic field vector used to orient subatomic particles.
* **Pull**: An attractive Coulomb force drawing negatively charged particles toward positive centers.
* **Electron**: A stable subatomic particle with a negative elementary charge that populates discrete orbital shells.

## 2. Worked Problem: Guiding Charged Particles into Potential Well Containment
In relational physics, guiding charged orbital particles into a confined potential well requires coordinated field manipulation.
* Instruction: `pull green ball inside box`
* Physical Scenario: An alpha particle target (represented as a green test sphere, mass 0.5kg) is deflected at position (1.2, 0.4). A reach electrode is located at (0.3, 0.1). A Faraday containment cage is located at (0.0, 0.6).
* Mechanics Execution: The operator applies the magnetic reach guide, exerts attractive force to bring the particle into the central detection aperture, and secures it inside the Faraday cage.

## 3. Relational Analogy: Classical Tabletop Orbits to Rutherford-Bohr Atomic Structure
The central-force mathematics governing solar system planetary orbits and tabletop tethered spheres map directly to nuclear models.
* Source Domain: Tabletop Tethered Sphere and Central Fulcrum.
* Target Domain: Rutherford-Bohr Hydrogen Atom (Proton Nucleus and Orbiting Electron Shell).
* Structural Mapping: Inverse-square gravitational force maps to inverse-square Coulomb electrostatic attraction; central mass maps to nuclear core.
